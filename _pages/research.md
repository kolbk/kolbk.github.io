**Rising waters, falling well-being: The effects of the 2013 East German flood on subjective well-being.** \
*(Environmental and Resource Economics, accepted/forthcoming)* \
with [Sachintha Fernando](https://sachi-fernando.github.io/) (MLU Halle) and [Christoph Wunder](https://oekonometrie.wiwi.uni-halle.de/team/prof._dr._christoph_wunder/) (MLU Halle)
<details markdown="1">
<summary> Abstract</summary>

This paper employs a panel event study design to examine the causal effects of the 2013 flood disaster in East Germany on subjective well-being. We merge geo-spatial flood data with longitudinal data from the Socio-Economic Panel (SOEP) to identify individuals in affected municipalities. Our results show that those affected by the flood report a significant life satisfaction drop of 0.17 points on an 11-point scale, which is equivalent to a 2.5% fall from pre-flood levels, in the year after the flood. The effect is more severe in peripheral areas than in central areas, and for low-income individuals than for high-income individuals. However, the effect dissipates by 2015. Additionally, we observe a notable initial decrease in health satisfaction, followed by recovery, while financial satisfaction was largely unaffected. 

</details>

# Working Papers

**No Ripple in the Pond: Exposure to Refugees and Native Well‑Being.** \
*(draft available upon request)* \
with [Kamal Kassam](https://kamalkassam.com/) (Universidad Carlos III de Madrid)
<details>
<summary> Abstract</summary>

The number of refugees fleeing global conflicts and crises is steadily increasing, making it crucial to understand their impact on the host countries. This paper examines the impact of a large-scale refugee inflow into Germany on the subjective well-being of natives. We combine county-level administrative records on recently arrived refugees with individual-level survey data from the German Socio-Economic Panel (SOEP) for the period 2014 to 2019. To identify causal effects, we employ a shift-share instrumental variable strategy based on a regional refugee allocation policy. Despite societal polarization over immigration and skepticism about refugee integration, we do not find a statistically or economically significant impact of local refugee inflows on natives' life satisfaction. This near-zero effect remains consistent across detailed heterogeneity analyses and multiple sensitivity checks. The findings of this study suggest that local exposure to refugees does not disproportionately burden specific groups or regions, thereby highlighting the adaptive capacity of host communities in the face of large-scale migration shocks.

</details>



**From Heat to Strain? The Impact of Heatwaves on Subjective Well-Being** \
*(draft available upon request)* \
with Felix Zwies (University Bayreuth)
<details>
<summary> Abstract</summary>

Extreme heat events are becoming more frequent and intense under climate change, increasingly affecting countries not traditionally exposed to extreme heat. This paper examines the impact of heatwaves on subjective well-being in Germany—a temperate, highly urbanized, and industrialized country, which reflects conditions in many advanced economies. Using individual-level panel data from the German Socio-Economic Panel (SOEP) combined with high-resolution weather data from the German Meteorological Service (DWD), we examine the relationship between heatwaves and life satisfaction using a panel fixed effects framework.
Our results show that heatwave exposure has a detrimental impact on life satisfaction, with a reduction of 0.06 points on an 11-point scale. This adverse effect is transitory, it persists until two weeks after the event and then dissipates. Further analyses reveal that heatwaves negatively affect specific life domains, most notably satisfaction with sleep, family, and health, while also increasing concerns about climate change impacts and the environmental protection. 
These results highlight the strains of heatwaves and emphasize the need for targeted climate change adaptation, especially for urban and vulnerable populations.
